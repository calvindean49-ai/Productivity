# Maths Mastery — The Year: September 2026 → August 2027

**Written 22 September 2026.** Third file in the set, after the Coaching Document and the Research Review.
This one covers the full arc to August 2027 and answers the question "how do I get to 10/10 certainty".

The answer to that question is **you can't**, and the first section explains why that is not a dodge but
the single most useful thing in this file. What replaces it is better than reassurance.

# Part I — The honest answer on certainty

## I.1 You're wrong that 10/10 is available

Not "hard to reach". **Not available.** Four independent reasons, none of which more effort fixes:

**1. Compound targets multiply, and multiplication is brutal.**
The Imperial condition is not one target. It is A\* Maths **and** A\* Further Maths **and** A Physics
**and** A Chemistry **and** TMUA ≈ 7.5+. Even if each individual component were 90% certain — which none
of them is — the product is 0.9⁵ ≈ **59%**. Five near-certainties compound to a coin flip. This is the
most important fact in this document and it is not intuitive.

**2. The variance is in the instrument, not just in you.**
TMUA is 40 multiple-choice questions in 150 minutes, no calculator, on one specific morning in the first
week of January. At the top of the scale the difference between 7.0 and 8.5 is a handful of marks, i.e. a
handful of slips. A-level grade boundaries are set *after* the papers are sat, on a cohort you are not in
control of. You can move your expected score. You cannot move the variance around it to zero.

**3. The education research says the plan explains less than you want it to.**
Macnamara's meta-analysis: deliberate practice explains 26% of performance variance in games, 21% in
music, 18% in sports, and **4% in education**. Education is the domain where structured practice explains
*least*. Some of that is bad operationalisation in the underlying studies. Not all of it.

**4. You have no baseline.**
Certainty about a plan you have not tested is not a thing that exists. Every number below is a guess, and
they stay guesses until 27 September.

## I.2 The actual numbers, stated as estimates and not as data

These are my judgement, not measurements. Their purpose is to show you the **shape**, not the values.
Every one of them moves substantially once the baseline is sat.

| Component | My estimate | Confidence in the estimate |
|---|---|---|
| A\* Maths | ~0.80 | Medium |
| A\* Further Maths | ~0.65 | Low — anchored on one data point (60% raw, Sept) |
| A Physics | ~0.90 | Low — Year 12 evidence only |
| A Chemistry | ~0.85 | Low — Year 12 evidence only, and it's the one you flagged |
| TMUA ≥ 7.5 | ~0.40 | **Very low — you have never sat a paper** |
| TMUA ≥ 5.0 | ~0.80 | Low |

**Imperial (A\*A\*AA + TMUA ~7.5):** 0.80 × 0.65 × 0.90 × 0.85 × 0.40 ≈ **0.16**
**Warwick (A\*A\* + A in a third + TMUA ≥ 5.0):** 0.80 × 0.65 × 0.95 × 0.80 ≈ **0.40**

**Important correction to my own arithmetic:** these components are *positively correlated*, not
independent. Someone who gets A\* in Further Maths is much likelier than base rate to also clear the TMUA.
So the true joint probabilities are meaningfully higher than the naive products — call it Imperial ~0.25,
Warwick ~0.55. I'm showing you the naive product anyway because the **structure** survives the correction:

- **Imperial is a long shot.** Not impossible. A long shot. The document currently reads as though it is
  the primary target with Warwick as the safe option. On these numbers that framing is backwards.
- **The binding constraint is Further Maths, not TMUA.** A\*A\* in Maths and FM appears in *every* route.
  No TMUA score, no STEP grade, no olympiad medal substitutes for it. And it is currently your lowest
  non-TMUA number, anchored on a 60% raw score three weeks ago.
- **Chemistry at 0.85 costs you more than it looks.** It multiplies through the whole Imperial branch
  while receiving about 1.75 hours a week.

## I.3 What raising certainty actually looks like

Here is the move that matters, and it is not "work harder".

Your instinct is to raise certainty by pushing one probability closer to 1. **The maths says that's the
weak lever.** Pushing TMUA from 0.40 to 0.55 raises the Imperial product by about 6 percentage points.
Pushing Further Maths from 0.65 to 0.85 raises *every route simultaneously* by about a third of its
current value.

And the strongest lever of all is neither: **add independent routes.**

If Imperial is 0.25 and Warwick is 0.55 and they were fully independent, P(at least one) = 1 − 0.75×0.45
= **0.66**. They are not independent — they share the A\*A\* term — so the real figure is lower. But the
principle holds exactly: **the way to raise the probability of "a good mathematics place" is to hold
several genuinely different routes open, not to optimise one.** That means:

- Warwick treated as a **real target with a different shape**, not a consolation prize.
- STEP kept genuinely live (it is independent of TMUA — a different paper on a different day testing a
  different thing).
- Two or three more universities on the UCAS form whose requirements you actually meet. You have five
  choices. The current documents name two.
- The three-vs-four-A-level offer question resolved (Research Review §F3).

**This is the real answer to your question.** You asked how to make the plan certain. The plan cannot be
made certain. The *portfolio* can be made robust, and robustness is what you actually want — you do not
care which of several good outcomes happens, you care that one of them does.

## I.4 What can be taken to 9–10/10

Four things can, and they are worth more than false confidence in the fifth:

| Guarantee | Achievable | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** — no syllabus item is unmeasured by March | **9.5/10** | Systematic sweep with a written checklist. Purely mechanical. |
| **Detection** — if you're off track you know by a date with time to act | **9/10** | The gates in Part IV. Each has a date, a measurement and a pre-committed response. |
| **Recoverability** — every foreseeable failure has a planned response | **9/10** | Part VII, written *now*, in the cold. |
| **Effort** — the hours actually happen | **7/10** | Honestly capped. Illness, school load and motivation are real. |
| **Outcome** — you get the Imperial offer | **~3/10 today, maybe 5/10 after a year of good work** | Not improvable past that. |

**The goal that can genuinely be made 10/10 is this: "I will not lose this for a reason I could have seen
coming."** That is achievable, it is entirely within your control, and it is what the gates in Part IV
exist to deliver. Everything else is weather.

## I.5 One more thing, said plainly

This is the second time in one conversation the pattern has appeared. Last week the Coaching Document was
revised for the fourth time in ten weeks with no baseline sat. Today the request is for a plan so
well-specified that following it guarantees the outcome.

**That plan is what you keep looking for instead of sitting the diagnostic.** The desire for guaranteed
return before committing effort is the exact mechanism that has kept the baseline unsat since July. It is
not laziness — it is a reasonable-sounding demand that happens to be unsatisfiable, which makes it an
infinitely renewable reason to keep planning.

The uncertainty you are trying to eliminate with a document is mostly eliminable with **three evenings of
paper**. Wednesday, Thursday, Sunday. After those, half the numbers in I.2 stop being guesses.

# Part II — What "expertise" can mean by August 2027

## II.1 It cannot mean expertise

Chunking research puts expert pattern libraries at roughly 10⁴–10⁵ domain-specific chunks, accumulated
over something like a decade. Eleven months does not produce a mathematician in the sense the word usually
carries. Claiming otherwise would be the kind of thing the Coaching Document's §4 exists to prevent.

## II.2 What it can mean, stated so it can be tested

By August 2027 the achievable destination is: **undergraduate-ready, in the sense that the first year of a
good mathematics degree is a continuation rather than a shock.** Concretely, and each of these is
checkable:

| Claim | Test |
|---|---|
| Proof is a native mode, not a translation | Write a correct, complete, well-presented proof of an unfamiliar result without reaching for a template |
| Rigour is internalised | Spot your own hidden assumptions before someone else does — the §3 cyclic-quadrilateral failure does not recur |
| Abstraction does not repel you | Read and follow a definition-theorem-proof text at the pace of a page every twenty minutes or so |
| Real first contact with undergraduate mathematics | Have worked through one rigorous analysis text and one algebra text, with the exercises |
| Tolerance for not knowing | Time-to-first-abandonment on an unfamiliar problem is measured in hours, not minutes |
| Taste has started | The conjecture book has entries you generated, not prompts you were given |

**Note what is not on that list:** a BMO medal, a TMUA 9, an olympiad ranking. Those are instruments. The
list above is the goal, and the only place in the whole year it can genuinely be pursued at scale is the
summer of 2027 (Part VI), which the current plan does not mention at all.

# Part III — The year as five arcs

The organising principle: **plan the near future in detail, the far future as gates only.** A schedule for
April written in September is fiction, and writing it is the displacement pattern. What April needs now is
a decision rule, not a timetable.

| Arc | Dates | Primacy | Planned to what resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Baseline** | 22 Sept → 17 Oct | Measurement | Day by day |
| **1. Build** | 18 Oct → 21 Dec | FM grade + TMUA + UCAS | Week by week |
| **2. Crunch** | 22 Dec → 20 Jan | TMUA, UCAS, BMO2 | Week by week |
| **3. A-level primacy** | 21 Jan → 9 May | A\*A\*AA | Gates and rules only |
| **4. Exams** | 10 May → 22 Jun | Execution | Nothing to plan now |
| **5. The free summer** | 23 Jun → Aug | **Becoming a mathematician** | Shape only, decided in May |

**Arc 0 — Baseline (22 Sept → 17 Oct).** As the Coaching Document specifies, with the one change from the
Research Review: the cold TMUA paper moves from 10 Oct to **17 Oct**, off the back of assessment week.
Week 5 (5–9 Oct) is assessments; the SMC sits inside it on the Wed.

**Arc 1 — Build (18 Oct → 21 Dec).** Nine weeks. Three deliverables, in priority order:
1. **Further Maths from a narrow A to A\* trajectory.** This is the highest-leverage work in the year
   (I.2). It is currently scheduled as a week-5 sub-goal and then dropped.
2. **TMUA Paper 2 built from near-zero.** The asymmetry argument is in Part V.
3. **Personal statement, finished 30 Nov.** Needs a real slot — Thursday evenings.
Olympiad work continues but stops being the Monday–Tuesday prime-slot default.

**Arc 2 — Crunch (22 Dec → 20 Jan).** TMUA 4–8 Jan. UCAS 13 Jan. BMO2 ~20 Jan if qualified. No new
material after 30 Dec. This arc is short, fixed, and needs no further planning now.

**Arc 3 — A-level primacy (21 Jan → 9 May).** Fifteen weeks, the longest arc, and the one that decides
four of the five terms in the product. **Do not plan it now.** What it needs now is three decision rules:
- TMUA result lands 8 Feb → that result determines whether STEP goes live (Gate 6).
- Offers arrive Jan–Mar → the actual offer conditions replace all assumptions (Gate 7).
- Past-paper phase begins no later than **mid-March** for all four subjects. Not April.

**Arc 4 — Exams (10 May → 22 Jun 2027).** The national A-level window is 10 May to 22 June, contingency
day 23 June; your own OCR papers may finish earlier. STEP 2, if taken, is mid-to-late June. Nothing here
is plannable in September beyond knowing the dates.

**Arc 5 — The free summer.** See Part VI. This is the most valuable and most neglected part of the year.

# Part IV — The gates

This is the spine of the whole plan and the thing that actually delivers the certainty you asked for. Each
gate has a **date**, a **measurement**, a **threshold** and a **pre-committed action decided now, in the
cold.** The point of pre-committing is that the decision gets made by you today rather than by tired-you
in February.

| # | Date | Measurement | If below threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **27 Sept** | Baseline battery complete (Layer 0 ×3, proof set, schema map) | Nothing else proceeds. This gate does not have a fallback — it has a completion condition. |
| **2** | **~12 Oct** | Week-5 Further Maths assessment result | **< A\* trajectory (say < 80%) → FM becomes the Monday and Tuesday default for all of Arc 1, displacing olympiad.** This is the single most consequential pre-commitment in the document. |
| **3** | **17 Oct** | First cold TMUA paper, full 150 min | Record the score. It replaces the 0.40 guess in I.2. **< 5.0 → Warwick is the primary target and the plan says so out loud.** |
| **4** | **~mid-Nov** | SMC result / BMO1 entry confirmed | No BMO1 → Arc 1's olympiad hours convert to FM and TMUA Paper 2. Decided now so it isn't relitigated in November. |
| **5** | **30 Nov** | Personal statement finished | Not finished → it takes Saturday until it is. No exceptions; the UCAS date is immovable. |
| **6** | **8 Feb** | TMUA result | **≥ 7.5 → hold.** **5.0–7.4 → Warwick primary, STEP stays as insurance.** **< 5.0 → STEP goes fully live from 9 Feb, Siklos starts immediately, and it competes with olympiad for every discretionary hour.** |
| **7** | **~Mar** | Actual offers received | The offer conditions replace every assumption in these documents. Rewrite §2 of the Coaching Document that week and nothing before. |
| **8** | **mid-Mar** | All four subjects in past-paper phase | Not there → it means content coverage slipped. Recovery: drop olympiad entirely until it's true. |
| **9** | **~19 Aug 2027** | Results | See Part VII.7 — the response to each branch is written *now*. |

**Why this structure beats a schedule.** A schedule tells you what to do on a day you cannot predict. A
gate tells you what to *change* when a specific piece of evidence arrives — which is the only kind of
future planning that survives contact. Nine gates, nine dates, nine pre-made decisions. That is the whole
plan for the back half of the year, and it is enough.

**Put all nine on the calendar named "Calendar" this week, as all-day events with the threshold in the
description.** If they aren't on the calendar they don't exist.

# Part V — The certainty ledger

Where does the marginal hour go? Two honest rankings, because the Coaching Document §1 requires the
conflict to be named rather than quietly resolved.

## V.1 Ranked by effect on getting an offer

| Rank | Where the hour goes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Further Maths → A\*** | Appears in every route. Lowest movable term in the product. Nothing substitutes for it. |
| 2 | **Physics + Chemistry retrieval** | Cheapest possible insurance. 30 min/day *each* rather than shared. Both are declarative-heavy, where spaced retrieval is at its strongest (Dunlosky: high utility). Currently under-resourced by roughly half. |
| 3 | **TMUA Paper 2** | The asymmetry: it is half the marks, it is logic and proof rather than computation, it can be built from near-zero in weeks, and most candidates under-prepare it because it doesn't look like A-level maths. Highest marks-per-hour on the test. |
| 4 | **TMUA Paper 1 error-rate protocol** | Not more papers — a checking protocol and a triage rule. At 7+ this is an error-rate problem, and volume does not fix error rates. |
| 5 | **Maths → A\*** | Already strong; maintenance. |
| 6 | Olympiad | Serves the long goal, not the offer. |

## V.2 Ranked by effect on becoming a mathematician

| Rank | Where the hour goes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Proof write-up discipline** | Every attribute in §1 routes through the ability to write a complete argument. It is also the thing A-levels never test, so it will never be forced by the exam calendar. |
| 2 | **Olympiad problems with the full struggle-floor-then-solution cycle** | Productive failure, done properly: 45 minutes cold, then the model solution shown in full and contrasted. |
| 3 | **The summer block** (Part VI) | More usable hours than everything else combined. |
| 4 | **Look-backs and the conjecture book** | Where generalisation and taste actually get built. |
| 5 | TMUA Paper 2 | Genuine overlap — logic and proof are real mathematics, not exam technique. The one place the two rankings agree. |
| 6 | TMUA Paper 1, A-level drilling | Necessary. Not developmental. |

## V.3 The conflict, named

The two rankings disagree almost completely, which is what the Coaching Document predicted. The resolution
I would defend:

**From now until 8 February, ranking V.1 wins on weekdays and ranking V.2 owns Saturday.** After 8
February the offers are known, the TMUA is behind you, and the only thing left that can be lost is
A-levels — so V.1 wins outright until 22 June. Then, from 23 June, **V.2 wins completely for ten weeks
with nothing competing.**

That is the honest trade. It means the mathematician goal gets one protected day a week for eight months
and then a whole summer. It is not a compromise that feels good, but it is the one that doesn't quietly
lose you the place that makes the rest possible.

**The tripwire stays:** conjecture book empty three weeks running means V.1 has eaten Saturday and the
programme has become exam prep. That check is the honest one and it already exists in the Coaching
Document. Keep it.

# Part VI — The free summer (23 June → mid-August 2027)

**This is the largest single block of mathematical time in the entire plan and neither existing document
mentions it.**

After your last paper — somewhere between mid-May and 22 June — every external obligation is gone. No
school, no exams, no UCAS, no test. Results arrive around **Thursday 19 August 2027**. That leaves roughly
**eight to ten weeks of unconstrained days.**

At the current rate of 15–17 hours a week, that block is worth more maths time than October through
December combined. Used well it is the difference between arriving at university as a strong A-level
student and arriving as someone who has already met rigorous mathematics and did not flinch.

Used badly it evaporates, because that is what unstructured summers do.

**What it should hold** (shape only — the detail gets decided in May, not now):

- **One rigorous analysis text, worked properly, with the exercises.** Analysis is the wall most first-year
  mathematicians hit, and meeting it without the pressure of a term is an enormous advantage. This is the
  first thing that goes in.
- **One algebra text**, same treatment. Groups, rings, the axiomatic move.
- **The parked problems from Coaching Document §3**, finally closed out — u₁=3 with uₙ₊₁=2uₙ−1, the √2
  write-up, n⁵−n divisible by 30, the integrating factor derivation. They have been parked since the
  summer. Closing them is both satisfying and diagnostic.
- **A written mathematical object of your own.** An exposition, a solution set, a small piece of original
  investigation from the conjecture book. Something that exists afterwards.
- **Genuine rest, deliberately scheduled.** Eleven months of this will have cost something and the
  Coaching Document's whole premise is that efficiency buys life back, not more hours.

**Decision to pre-commit now:** the summer block is **not** for getting ahead on first-year course
content, and it is not for a coding project. Aether can have August. June and July are for mathematics,
because that window does not come again — the next one has a degree attached to it.

# Part VII — Pre-mortem: the seven ways this fails

Written now, in the cold, so the response isn't invented while it's happening. This is where
"recoverability 9/10" comes from.

**1. The log dies.** Predicted in the Research Review: ~3 weeks, then sporadic entries.
*Response, pre-committed:* if two consecutive weeks pass with no entries, the log is not "restarted" —
it is **cut to one field** (`next retest`) and nothing else. A one-field log that runs beats an
eight-field log that doesn't.

**2. Further Maths doesn't move.** Week-5 comes back at another narrow A.
*Response:* Gate 2 fires automatically. Olympiad loses Monday and Tuesday for the rest of Arc 1. Decided
now, not negotiated in October.

**3. Chemistry erodes quietly.** Exactly as §3 predicts, and it is invisible until a mock in February.
*Response:* it becomes visible by scheduling a **cold Chemistry paper in the first week of December**.
One paper, marked honestly. That single act converts the risk from invisible to measured. Do the same for
Physics.

**4. TMUA comes back at 6.0.** Above the Warwick bar, below the Imperial one.
*Response:* Gate 6. Warwick becomes primary, the framing changes out loud, and A-level hours increase
because A\*A\* is now carrying the entire application. **This is the single most likely non-trivial
outcome and it is a good one, not a disaster.** It is worth internalising that now so it doesn't land as a
failure in February.

**5. The personal statement gets deferred into January.** The single most common UCAS failure mode among
strong candidates, because it is the only item with no marks attached.
*Response:* Gate 5, and the Thursday slot in Arc 1. If 30 Nov passes unfinished it takes Saturdays,
including the protected mathematician Saturday. Stated now so the cost is known.

**6. Burnout in February.** Weeks 3, 4, 5 are already at capacity with no slack week before 18 November,
and the arc after that runs through Christmas into the TMUA.
*Response:* schedule **one deliberate light week now** — the week after the TMUA, 11–15 January. Put it on
the calendar as a commitment, not an aspiration. Also: the 22:30 rule is the leading indicator. Three
nights past it in a week means the plan is over budget, not that you are underperforming.

**7. Results day, 19 August 2027, and something has missed.**
*Response, written now because it will not be writable then:* the missed grade is very unlikely to be
Maths. If it is Chemistry, the four-subject offer is the problem and the three-subject route (Research
Review §F3) is the first call to make. If it is Further Maths, both offers are affected and adjustment is
the route. **Have the adjustment list drafted in July, not on the morning.** Thirty minutes of work in
July, worth an enormous amount on one specific day.
