## 2. What this means, stated without softening

**Further Maths enters the outcome twice.**

- **Once before the offer.** UCAS goes in by 13 January carrying your predictions. At this level of
  competition, an applicant predicted *below* the standard offer condition is at a serious disadvantage.
  Departments that receive many more qualified applicants than places routinely use the prediction as a
  filter. Below-requirement offers happen, but chiefly through contextual schemes, not on merit alone.
- **Once after it.** Even given an offer, A\* in Further Maths is the grade you then have to produce.

Everything else has resolved in your favour. The Year Plan (§I.2) weighted Chemistry as a live risk —
**that was wrong, and I'm correcting it: a predicted A\* against a required A is comfortable, and the
"Chemistry erodes quietly" pre-mortem (Part VII.3) drops from a red flag to a December spot-check.**
Physics likewise. The five-term product I showed you has collapsed to essentially one term.

That is good news in one specific and important sense: **the problem is no longer diffuse.** It is one
subject, with one date, thirteen days away.

## 3. The revised gate

**Gate 2 in the Year Plan fires too late.** It was written to trigger *after* the week-5 result. The
decision that matters happens *before* it. Replace it:

> **Gate 2 (amended). Now → 9 October. Further Maths has priority over everything else that is not
> compulsory school work.** The week-5 Further Maths paper is the highest-stakes single event in the
> year, because it sets the prediction that determines whether offers are made at all.
>
> After the result: **A\* → resume the Year Plan as written.** **Below A\* → the immediate action is a
> conversation with your Further Maths teacher about what evidence would move the prediction before
> January, not a revision of any of these documents.**

That last clause matters. Predictions are set by teachers on evidence. If week 5 falls short, the
question is not "how do I study more", it is "what is the next piece of evidence that can move this, and
when is it". That is a five-minute conversation and it is worth more than fifty hours of solo work.

**Find out this week what the week-5 Further Maths paper covers.** Ask your teacher. Two minutes. Every
hour between now and 5 October is worth several times more if it is aimed at the right topics.

## 5. What is now under-reacted to, not over-reacted to

One correction in the other direction, so this file isn't only good news.

**The TMUA is still entirely unmeasured**, and it is the other term that gates Imperial. Nothing about the
predicted grades touches it. The 17 October paper stays exactly where it is and stays non-negotiable.

And **a predicted A\* is weaker evidence than it feels.** National data: only around 16% of applicants
receive accurate predictions and roughly three quarters are over-predicted; about half of UK 18-year-olds
were predicted AAA or better for 2025 entry while only 26% of accepted applicants achieved it.
Predictions are the currency for *getting* an offer. They are poor evidence for *meeting* one. Your three
A\* predictions are doing real work in January and should not be mistaken for three secured grades in
August.
